Screen designs · v1 scope · for review

Anthem
StudentsThe Week Hub

One link, sent every Thursday, that carries a student through the week between Wednesday nights. Not an app to download — a page to open. Below is every screen in v1, plus the leader tools behind it.

No student login Mobile first 3-day path Publishes in ~15 min/week

The problem

Wednesday night lands, then six days of nothing. Students have no structure between meetings and nowhere to go when they want one.

The bet

Students won't install an app. They will tap a link in the group chat if it opens instantly and asks for five minutes, not thirty.

The guardrail

Three days, not five. A student who finishes 3 of 3 comes back. A student who finishes 2 of 5 quits.

The leader test

If publishing a week takes longer than a coffee break, it stops happening by week six. The admin tool is built around that constraint.

Screen 01 · Home

This Week

/

The whole product lives on this screen. A student taps the Thursday link and lands here — no sign-in, no onboarding, no tour. Everything that matters is above the fold.

  • Big idea — one sentence, set large. This is the thing you want stuck in their head all week, and it's the first thing they read.
  • Memory verse card — tappable, opens the practice screen.
  • The path — three day cards with completion dots. Progress is visible the second the page loads, which is the whole reason they come back.
  • Recap — collapsed by default. It's a safety net for the student who missed Wednesday, not the main event.
  • Parent link — quiet, at the bottom. Parents mostly arrive by their own link.
Why it's built this way

Every element competing for the top of the screen loses to the big idea. If a student only ever reads one line all week, this design makes sure it's the right line.

Screen 02 · Daily

A Day On The Path

/day/1 · /day/2 · /day/3

One screen, one job. Read a short passage, hear one honest thought, answer one question. Four minutes, not a quiet time curriculum.

  • Full passage on screen. Never ask a middle schooler to go find a Bible — that's the step where they close the tab.
  • One question. Not three. Three questions reads like homework and gets zero answers.
  • Journal saves to their phone only. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is readable by leaders. That's a promise we make on the screen, and it's the reason answers will be honest.
  • Scanlines dial down here. Nobody types something vulnerable through a CRT filter.
  • "I'm done" returns them home with a dot filled in. The reward is immediate and visible.
Why it's built this way

The single biggest predictor of a student finishing is how much the screen asks of them at once. One passage, one thought, one question is the most we can ask and still get day three.

Screen 03 · Practice

Verse Trainer

/verse

The verse blanks out progressively across four levels — full text, a few words gone, most words gone, reference only. Tap any blank to peek. Level up when you're ready.

  • This is the repeat-open screen. Everything else is read once. This one gets tapped Friday in a car line and Sunday before service.
  • Streak lives here, not on the home screen — tied to practice, where daily repetition actually makes sense.
  • Works as the small-group opener on Wednesday: put it on the projector at level 3 and let the room fill it in.
Why it's built this way

It's the only screen in v1 that behaves like a game, and that's deliberate — memorization is the one thing here that genuinely rewards repetition. Build it well and it carries the whole product's return rate.

Screen 04 · Navigation

The Menu

Overlay · all screens

Five destinations. That's the ceiling — the moment this list needs scrolling, the product has lost its shape.

  • Each item carries a live subtitle (3 coming up, Level 2, 11 in the archive) so the menu itself tells you what's worth tapping.
  • "Reduce screen effects" is not decoration. Scanlines and glow trigger headaches for some students, and this is an accessibility obligation as much as a preference. It also respects the phone's own reduced-motion setting automatically.
Why it's built this way

A hamburger is where features go to be forgotten. Anything genuinely important gets pulled onto the home screen instead — which is exactly why the daily path isn't in here.

Screen 05 · Events

What's Coming Up

/events

A clean list of everything on the calendar, with the one detail that actually matters on each card: cost, deadline, what to bring.

  • Sign-ups link out to whatever registration you already use. This screen doesn't take payments and shouldn't try to.
  • Deadlines are the point. "Deposit due Sep 12" in front of a student in September is worth more than an announcement slide in August.
  • Doubles as the answer to every "wait, when is that?" text a leader gets.
Why it's built this way

Scope honesty: this screen only earns its place because it's cheap to build on the same content model. If v1 has to shrink, this is the first thing to cut — the weekly path is the product.

Screen 06 · Archive

Past Weeks

/archive

Every published week, grouped by series, staying live forever. Old weeks open exactly as they did when they were current.

  • For the student who was gone — camp, a game, a rough month — and wants to catch up without asking anyone.
  • Their completion history stays visible, which quietly turns eleven weeks of scattered effort into something that looks like a track record.
  • It's the leader's library too. Every series you've ever taught, searchable, with the verse and the big idea attached.
Why it's built this way

This screen costs almost nothing to build — the weeks are already in the database — and it's the only thing that makes the product feel bigger the longer it runs.

Screen 07 · Parents

Parent Guide

/parents

What we covered, one heads-up, three conversation starters, and the verse their kid is memorizing. Under a minute to read.

  • The neon comes off. Warm gold instead of pink, calmer type, no VHS treatment. This audience needs to trust it in four seconds, and a retro filter reads as unserious to a forty-year-old.
  • The heads-up block is the differentiator. When a hard topic gets named on a Wednesday, a parent finding out from this page instead of from a blindside is the single most valuable thing the product does.
  • Questions are written to survive a car ride — specific, answerable, not "so what did you learn."
  • No login. Parents don't create accounts for church websites.
Why it's built this way

Be honest about who actually opens links: this page will likely out-engage the student pages, and it's what parents forward to other parents. It's the growth channel, not a bonus feature.

Screen 08 · Admin

Leader Login

/admin

Email and password for a handful of leaders. No student accounts exist anywhere in the system, which is the single largest reduction in build cost, privacy risk, and ongoing support burden available to this project.

  • Two roles. Editor can draft; Publisher can push live. On a team this size that's plenty.
  • No minor data is ever collected — no names, no emails, no journal entries leaving the phone. Worth stating plainly to leadership; it takes a whole category of child-safety and data-retention questions off the table.

Screen 09 · Admin

Week Editor

/admin/week/:id

Every field for one week on one screen. No tabs, no wizard, no save button to forget — it autosaves as you type and stays a draft until someone deliberately publishes it.

  • Draft / Live is the only state that matters. A half-written week can never be visible to a student, which means you can edit Thursday morning without fear.
  • Scheduled publish — write it Wednesday night while it's fresh, let it go live Thursday at 7 AM on its own.
  • "Duplicate last week" carries the series, formatting, and structure forward so you're editing rather than starting from a blank page. Blank pages are why weekly content dies.
  • "Preview as student" shows the real phone screen before anything goes out.
Why it's built this way

Every field here maps one-to-one to something visible on the student screens. Nothing is collected "for later." A field that doesn't render is a field someone has to fill in for no reason, every week, forever.

Screen 10 · Admin

Start From Your Notes

/admin/week/new

Paste the lesson notes that already exist for Wednesday night, and the week comes back roughly 80% filled in. You edit and publish rather than write from scratch.

  • This is the feature that decides whether the product survives. Four original pieces of content a week, forever, is not a workload anyone sustains on top of a full ministry job. Deriving from notes that already exist is.
  • It never publishes on its own. Everything lands in the editor as a draft for a human to fix. The tool drafts; the leader decides.
  • Skippable — a leader who'd rather type it fresh clicks past it.
Why it's built this way

Most church tools die of content starvation, not bad design. This screen attacks the actual failure mode instead of adding another one.

Screen 11 · Admin

Publish & Send

/admin/week/:id/published

Publishing isn't finished when the page goes live — it's finished when the link is in the group chat. This screen closes that gap.

  • The message is pre-written and one click from your clipboard, editable before sending. Removing friction from the send is worth more than any feature on the student side.
  • QR code for the projector on Wednesday night — how new students find it the first time.
  • Four numbers. Not a dashboard. Opens, last week's opens, parent opens, and how many reached day three.
Why it's built this way

"Reached day 3" is the honest metric — it's the one that says whether this is changing anyone's week or just getting clicked. Put it in front of leadership monthly. If it stays near zero after six weeks, the format is wrong and we change it rather than adding features.

04

Decide Before Build

Open questions

Six things that change the build

These are cheap to settle in a meeting and expensive to change once code exists. Everything above is designed to hold steady regardless of how they land — but the answers set the schedule.

01 · Distribution

What channel reliably reaches students on a Thursday? The site is worthless without a habit of sending it. This is the highest-risk item and it isn't a design problem.

02 · Translation

Which translation do students use, and do we have the licensing to display full passages? Public-domain fallback works if not.

03 · Who publishes

Is this one person's job or a rotation? A rotation needs the notes-paste feature more, not less.

04 · Domain

Its own short domain, or a path on the church site? Short and typo-proof matters more than being on-brand.

05 · Journal storage

Device-only is assumed here. It's safer, cheaper, and more honest. Changing that means accounts, consent, and minor-data policy.

06 · The kill signal

Agree now on the number that means this isn't working, and the date we check it. Six weeks, "reached day 3." Deciding that in advance is what keeps it from becoming a zombie.
